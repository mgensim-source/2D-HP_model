** Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) algorithm ** 

(a) Greedy Algorithm: 
Makes the single best local choice at each exact step without looking ahead or ever changing its mind. 
It is fast, but it often gets trapped in poor, sub-optimal end results (local minima).

(b) Heuristic based: 
A rule of thumb or educated guess used to quickly find a decent solution for a complex problem (like choosing the shortest visible path next).

(c) Ant Colony Optimization (ACO): 
A metaheuristic framework. It uses a mix of probabilistic choices, multi-agent collaboration, and global communication via virtual 
pheromone trails to explore many paths and iteratively improve them over time.

WHY THIS WORKS??????

> Heuristic (eta) — local, in-the-moment: "does this move touch an H right now?" Same rule every time, no memory of past runs.

> Pheromone (tau) — global, retrospective: applied after the full fold is done, based on total energy, to every move in that path — even ones that looked unremarkable at the time but led somewhere good.

> Heuristic alone = greedy random search, no way to learn from final outcomes (this is what you saw when deposit was removed — it plateaus immediately).

> Pheromone alone = works, but starts with zero guidance, so early iterations wander blind.

> Together: heuristic gives sensible bias from move 1, pheromone lets good full-fold outcomes reinforce the specific path that produced them over time. Same combo as classic ACO for TSP (distance heuristic + learned pheromone), just applied to folds instead of routes.
